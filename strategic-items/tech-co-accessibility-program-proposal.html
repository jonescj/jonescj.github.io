Executive summarydecision
Business problem

Accessibility is fragmented

Standards, training, testing, procurement, and remediation sit in separate functions. That creates inconsistent customer experiences, repeated defects, late rework, and unmeasured legal and commercial risk.

Proposal

Build a reusable operating model

Create a small central program that sets policy and evidence standards, while product teams retain responsibility for accessible design, delivery, and remediation.

Expected result

Shift from retrofit to prevention

Prioritize critical journeys, add accessibility controls to normal delivery workflows, and use customer evidence plus operational metrics to decide what scales.

Decision request

Approve a 24-month pilot-to-scale program, a 4.5 FTE central team, a $2.7M base budget, three pilot product journeys, and executive sponsorship with quarterly governance reviews.

Business problem & assumptions01

The issue is not a missing checklist. It is a missing operating model.

The source proposals covered websites, mobile applications, Bluetooth hardware interfaces, cloud software, multimedia, PDFs, communication platforms, kiosks, and learning systems. Treating all of those surfaces as one remediation backlog would be technically broad but strategically weak.

This proposal instead assumes that Tech Co. needs a way to make accessibility decisions repeatedly: which journeys matter first, who owns the work, what evidence is required before release, when an exception is acceptable, and how leadership knows whether risk and user barriers are declining.

Explicit assumptions
Organization

Global product ecosystem

Tech Co. operates public websites, iOS and Android apps, cloud tools, Bluetooth-connected products, support content, and third-party platforms.

Current state

No mature central function

Accessibility expertise exists unevenly, but there is no common policy, inventory, severity model, release evidence standard, or enterprise dashboard.

Delivery model

Teams keep product ownership

The central program supplies standards, tools, coaching, audit methods, and escalation. Product teams own implementation and defects in their roadmaps.

Budget boundary

Legacy rebuilds are separate

The base estimate funds the program and targeted remediation. It does not fund full replacement of inaccessible legacy platforms or major hardware redesigns.

How might we

How might we make accessibility a measurable part of how Tech Co. prioritizes, designs, ships, procures, and supports digital experiences, without creating a centralized bottleneck?

Program strategy02

The strategy has four connected layers: define expectations, prevent new barriers, remediate priority journeys, and learn from disabled users and operational evidence.

90-minute annual briefing, case-based risk workshop, quarterly dashboard review.

Proof: approves scope, exceptions, and investment using the priority model.

Product & design

Define accessible outcomes

Three-hour role lab, design critiques, acceptance-criteria templates, co-design sessions.

Proof: accessible PRD and prototype review on a live product.

Engineering

Build and prevent

Three-hour coding labs, semantic patterns, ARIA guidance, CI integration, office hours.

Proof: passing code review plus manual spot check on a representative flow.

QA

Validate beyond automation

Four-hour assistive-tech lab, severity calibration, pair testing, annual competency refresh.

Proof: completes an end-to-end keyboard and screen-reader test script.

Content & support

Create and respond

90-minute modules, accessible templates, captioning and document practice, escalation playbooks.

Proof: audited sample asset or correctly triaged accessibility ticket.

Legal & procurement

Buy and contract responsibly

Two-hour VPAT and contract lab, vendor evidence rubric, annual legal update.

Proof: documented review and risk disposition for a sample vendor.

Why this choice

Training is tied to a work product because completion is not competence. Generic awareness remains useful for onboarding, but it is intentionally not the primary evidence that a role can perform its accessibility responsibilities.

Primary risks and mitigations

Central team becomes a gatekeeper

Mitigation: product teams remain accountable, reusable patterns reduce consultation demand, and exceptions have time limits.

Audit creates an unpayable backlog

Mitigation: audit critical journeys first, separate urgent barriers from long-term debt, and cap work in progress.

Automation creates false confidence

Mitigation: no release is declared accessible from automated scans alone. Manual and disabled-user evidence remain required for priority journeys.

Disabled users are treated as final-stage validators

Mitigation: compensate panel members, involve them in pilot framing and prototype reviews, and publish how feedback changed decisions.

Vendor evidence is weak or misleading

Mitigation: validate high-risk claims, require remediation terms, and maintain a time-bound exception register.

Hardware scope overwhelms the program

Mitigation: begin with companion software and critical hardware interaction risks, then commission product-specific hardware studies where needed.

Why this choice

The roadmap starts with three end-to-end journeys rather than every asset. That creates visible customer outcomes and tests whether governance, training, tooling, and ownership work together before the company scales the model.

Measurement framework05

Measure outcomes, flow, adoption, and risk. Do not collapse them into one score.

The original evaluation plan proposed audits, user panels, training assessments, dashboards, stakeholder feedback, policy reviews, risk monitoring, and maturity assessments. The portfolio version turns those into a compact decision dashboard.

Reflection06

The strongest proposal is selective about what it promises.

The three source documents established a broad scope, a role-specific training model, a governance structure, a 24-month timeline, and a layered evaluation framework. The useful material was not the volume of standards or procedures. It was the underlying idea that accessibility must move from a one-time compliance exercise into a sustained organizational capability.

I therefore reframed the work around executive decisions, ownership, sequencing, evidence, and tradeoffs. The proposal does not claim that every Tech Co. asset will become conformant in 24 months. It proposes a system that can identify the most consequential barriers, prevent recurrence, and make investment choices visible.

What I intentionally excluded
  • A complete global legal matrix. Legal requirements change and depend on market, customer, and product context. Legal counsel should validate obligations; the portfolio case study should show the operating response.
  • Enterprise-wide remediation of every legacy asset. That promise is not credible without an inventory, architecture review, and product-level estimates.
  • Automated testing as a compliance proxy. Automation supports scale but cannot establish usability or full standards conformance.
  • One mandatory curriculum for everyone. Role-specific proof of competency is more useful than broad completion statistics.
  • Detailed hardware certification plans. Bluetooth hardware and embedded interfaces require product-specific engineering analysis that is beyond this enterprise operating-model proposal.
  • A public conformance claim in the first year. External claims should follow stable evidence, not precede it.
What this case study demonstrates
Proposal writing

Decisions before detail

The executive request, assumptions, budget, gates, and exclusions make the recommendation testable.

Service design

A system across functions

The work connects governance, product delivery, training, procurement, user research, support, and evidence.

Strategic restraint

Depth where it matters

The proposal prioritizes customer journeys and operating mechanisms instead of reproducing every standard and procedure.

Final reflection

Accessibility becomes durable when it is funded, owned, measured, and reviewed in the same systems that govern product quality, delivery risk, and customer experience.

A credible accessibility program does not promise perfection. It builds the capacity to improve, release after release.
